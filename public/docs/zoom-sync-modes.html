Understanding Zoom Sync Modes in MicStatusPro

MicStatusPro gives you flexible control over how your microphone works with Zoom. Whether you control your microphone with keyboard shortcuts, hardware buttons, or Zoom itself, MicStatusPro offers three sync modes to match your preferred workflow.

By default, MicStatusPro uses Two-Way Sync to keep Zoom and your microphone status synchronized. Read on to explore how each mode works and find the one that best fits your daily routine!

Recommended: Most users should keep the default Two-Way Sync mode. It automatically keeps Zoom and your microphone status synchronized in both directions.

Two-Way Sync (Default)

Two-Way Sync keeps your operating system and Zoom synchronized. When this mode is active, any change made on one side immediately updates the other.

  • How it works: Muting at the OS level (via hardware buttons, hotkeys, or MicStatusPro) automatically updates Zoom's mute status. Likewise, clicking Zoom's mute button immediately updates your microphone's operating-system mute status.
  • Pros: Everything stays seamlessly synchronized. Zoom and your computer always stay aligned, regardless of where the mute change originates.

OS Master

In OS Master mode, your computer controls Zoom's microphone status.

  • How it works: Whenever you mute or unmute using system shortcuts, Push-To-Talk, or MicStatusPro, the app clicks the corresponding button in Zoom for you. Changes only flow one way: from your computer to Zoom.
  • Pros: Ideal for users who primarily control their microphone with keyboard shortcuts or custom hotkeys.
  • Cons: If you manually click the mute button inside the Zoom window, Zoom will not push that status change back to your OS hardware, which can cause Zoom and your microphone status to become unsynchronized.

Zoom Master

In Zoom Master mode, Zoom becomes the primary controller of your microphone status.

  • How it works: Zoom controls your microphone's operating-system mute status. Any mute or unmute action performed in Zoom is automatically reflected by your computer.
  • Pros: Ideal for users who primarily use Zoom's mute button during meetings.
  • Cons: If you attempt to mute your hardware directly using MicStatusPro or system triggers, the request is ignored so Zoom remains in control of the microphone state.
